## Article 8 — Rights of citizenship of certain persons of Indian origin residing outside India

8. Rights of citizenship of certain persons of Indian origin residing
outside India.—Notwithstanding anything in article 5, any person who or
either of whose parents or any of whose grand-parents was born in India as
defined in the Government of India Act, 1935 (as originally enacted), and who
is ordinarily residing in any country outside India as so defined shall be deemed
to be a citizen of India if he has been registered as a citizen of India by the
diplomatic or consular representative of India in the country where he is for the
time being residing on an application made by him therefor to such diplomatic
or consular representative, whether before or after the commencement of this
Constitution, in the form and manner prescribed by the Government of the
Dominion of India or the Government of India.

### Plain English Summary
This article allows certain people who have Indian ancestry to become citizens of India even if they live in another country. If a person's parent or grandparent was born in India under the rules of the Government of India Act, 1935, and they live outside India, they can be considered an Indian citizen if they apply and get registered as such through an official representative of India in that foreign country.

### Key Points
* It deals with the citizenship rights of specific people of Indian origin living abroad.
* The condition is that the person or their parent/grandparent must have been born in India as defined by the Government of India Act, 1935.
* Citizenship is granted upon registration made by the diplomatic or consular representative of India in the country where the person resides.
* This process can happen either before or after the Constitution came into effect.

### Why It Matters
This article provides a pathway for people with Indian roots living overseas to secure their citizenship status in India through an official application process abroad.
